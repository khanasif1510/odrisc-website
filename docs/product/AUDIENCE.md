# 1. Purpose of This Document

This document defines the audiences served by the ODRISC website and establishes how each audience should be understood, prioritized, addressed, and converted.

It must guide:

* Website information architecture
* Patient and Provider navigation
* Page planning
* Content strategy
* Messaging
* Calls to action
* Interface design
* Visual hierarchy
* Product presentation
* Trust-building
* Search engine optimization
* Analytics
* Forms and lead routing
* Future personalization
* AI-assisted content and code generation

This document exists to prevent the Patient and Provider experiences from being mixed into one undifferentiated website journey.

The website must remain one unified ODRISC platform while recognizing that patients and healthcare providers have different needs, levels of knowledge, motivations, concerns, and conversion paths.

---

# 2. Audience Architecture

ODRISC serves two primary audience groups:

1. **Patients**
2. **Healthcare Providers**

These audiences enter through one website:

`odrisc.com`

The website entry experience asks visitors to identify whether they are:

* A Patient
* A Healthcare Provider

The selected audience determines:

* Homepage
* Navigation
* Content depth
* Terminology
* Product emphasis
* Trust signals
* Primary calls to action
* Conversion flow
* Recommended resources

Both audiences remain connected through the same ODRISC platform, brand, clinical philosophy, and longitudinal maternal-fetal intelligence framework.

---

# 3. Audience Priority

## 3.1 Equal Strategic Importance

Patients and providers are both primary audiences of the website.

Neither audience should be treated as a minor secondary visitor group.

The website must provide a complete and intentional journey for both.

## 3.2 Different Functional Roles

The Patient and Provider audiences are equally important but perform different roles in the ODRISC ecosystem.

Patients primarily:

* Learn
* Understand
* Assess
* Monitor
* Participate
* Engage
* Follow structured support
* Communicate with healthcare professionals

Providers primarily:

* Review
* Interpret
* Monitor
* Recognize
* Evaluate
* Collaborate
* Escalate when clinically appropriate
* Implement ODRISC in care workflows

## 3.3 Shared Outcome

Both audiences are connected through one shared outcome:

> Better-informed collaboration between patients and healthcare professionals to support improved maternal and fetal outcomes.

# 4. Patient Audience

## 4.1 Primary Patient Definition

The Patient journey is designed for women who are:

* Planning pregnancy
* Preparing for conception
* Preparing for IVF or assisted reproduction
* Currently pregnant
* Seeking greater understanding of pregnancy risk
* Monitoring weight or metabolic health
* Seeking structured nutrition support
* Seeking pregnancy-appropriate physical activity
* Interested in understanding maternal and fetal progress
* Looking for a more informed relationship with their healthcare provider

The term “Patient” is used as the website audience label, but the experience should remain respectful, supportive, and person-centered.

The website should not reduce users to diagnoses, risk scores, or clinical categories.

## 5.7 High-Risk Pregnancy Patients

### Description

Women who have been identified by their healthcare professional as requiring closer monitoring.

### Common Needs

* Clearer understanding of monitoring
* Better organization of reports and observations
* Structured communication with providers
* Confidence in when to contact the clinical team
* Reduced confusion between appointments

* Fetal growth timeline

### Content Requirement

The website must not encourage high-risk patients to self-manage serious clinical concerns without professional review.

## 5.8 Patient Supporters

### Description

Partners, spouses, and family members who may visit the Patient experience to understand how they can support someone planning pregnancy or currently pregnant.

### Common Needs

* Understanding the purpose of ODRISC
* Learning how to offer practical support
* Understanding risk without creating fear
* Supporting nutrition, activity, appointments, and monitoring
* Knowing when professional care is important

### Role in the Website

Supporters are a secondary audience within the Patient journey.

They should not displace women as the primary decision-makers and users of the Patient experience.

# 8. Patient Trust Requirements

Patient trust should be built through:

* Clear explanations
* Responsible clinical language
* Transparent limitations
* Qualified clinical and wellness team information
* Evidence references where relevant
* Strong privacy communication
* Non-judgmental language
* Product demonstrations
* Clear safety instructions
* Genuine founder motivation
* Testimonials only when authentic and approved

# 9. Patient Content Depth

Patient content should be understandable to a general audience.

Use:

* Plain language
* Short explanations
* Visual progression
* Simple diagrams
* Defined terminology
* Clear examples
* Layered detail
* Frequently asked questions
* Reassuring safety language

Avoid:

* Unexplained abbreviations
* Dense medical terminology
* Raw clinical data without interpretation
* Overly technical fetal-growth language
* Excessive statistics
* Long blocks of clinical evidence
* Alarmist language

Where clinical depth is necessary, use progressive disclosure:

```text
Simple explanation
↓
Why it matters
↓
What ODRISC helps monitor
↓
When to speak with a healthcare professional
↓
Optional detailed information
```

# 18. Audience Knowledge Levels

The website must account for varying levels of familiarity.

## 18.1 Patient Knowledge Levels

### Low Familiarity

Users may not understand:

* Gestational diabetes
* Gestational weight gain
* Fetal-growth percentiles
* Maternal metabolic risk
* Longitudinal monitoring

Content must begin with plain-language explanations.

### Moderate Familiarity

Users may know they have:

* PCOS
* Previous gestational diabetes
* IVF pregnancy
* High-risk pregnancy
* Weight-related concerns

Content should connect existing knowledge to practical monitoring.

### High Familiarity

Some users may be medically informed or already receiving specialist care.

The website should provide optional deeper information without making it the default experience.

## 18.2 Provider Knowledge Levels

### General Obstetric Care

Requires clear clinical value and workflow relevance.

### Specialist Care

Requires detailed methodology and advanced fetal-growth information.

### Administrative or Procurement

Requires implementation, security, commercial, governance, and scalability information.

### Allied Health

Requires role-specific capabilities and collaboration information.

The Provider experience should route each professional toward relevant content rather than presenting all information with equal prominence.

# 21. Audience Switching

Users must be able to switch between Patient and Provider journeys.

This is important because:

* A clinician may want to understand the Patient experience
* A patient may want to share Provider information with a doctor
* A hospital administrator may begin in the wrong journey
* A family member may need Patient content
* An allied-health professional may identify with both experiences

Audience switching must be:

* Clearly visible
* Easy to use
* Available on mobile
* Available without signing in
* Consistent across the website
* Measured through analytics

The switch must not create the impression of two unrelated brands.

# 26. Audience Content Rules for AI Agents

AI agents must identify the intended audience before generating:

* Copy
* Page layouts
* Components
* Calls to action
* Illustrations
* Diagrams
* Metadata
* Forms
* Navigation
* Product descriptions

Before generating audience-specific work, AI agents must establish:

1. Is this for Patients, Providers, or a shared audience?
2. Which audience segment is primary?
3. What does the visitor already understand?
4. What decision should the page support?
5. What clinical depth is appropriate?
6. Which claims are approved?
7. What action should the visitor take?

AI agents must not:

* Use provider terminology in Patient content without explanation
* Use consumer wellness language in clinical Provider pages
* Treat all pregnant women as high-risk
* Treat all providers as having identical needs
* Mix investor messaging into primary Patient or Provider journeys
* Invent additional audience categories for the gateway
* Add audience segments without documenting the reason
* Use fear, shame, or blame as conversion methods
* Assume roadmap capabilities are available

# 27. Current Audience Decisions

The following decisions are approved:

1. Patients and healthcare providers are both primary website audiences.
2. Visitors will select Patient or Provider at the entry experience.
3. Patients and providers will have separate audience-oriented journeys.
4. Both journeys will remain within `odrisc.com`.
5. Both journeys will use one unified brand and design system.
6. Patients will receive clear, supportive, non-judgmental content.
7. Providers will receive clinically credible, detailed, workflow-oriented content.
8. Patients must be encouraged to collaborate with healthcare professionals.
9. Providers must understand that ODRISC supports rather than replaces clinical judgment.
10. Users must be able to switch between journeys.
11. Investors and other corporate audiences will not be added to the primary gateway.
12. Weight-related content must avoid stigma and blame.
13. Audience-specific content must use different depth, terminology, trust signals, and calls to action.

# 29. Audience Success Criteria

The audience architecture is successful when:

## Patient Success

* Patients immediately recognize the experience is for them
* They understand what ODRISC does
* They feel supported rather than judged
* They understand that ODRISC does not replace medical care
* They can identify the relevant next action
* They understand why longitudinal monitoring matters
* They can explain ODRISC to their healthcare provider

## Provider Success

* Providers immediately recognize clinical relevance
* They understand the platform's role
* They can distinguish decision support from diagnosis
* They understand the longitudinal value
* They can identify workflow applications
* They can access evidence and methodology
* They can take a professional adoption action

## Shared Success

* Both audiences recognize the same ODRISC platform
* Audience switching is easy
* Messaging remains consistent
* The patient-provider relationship is strengthened
* The website does not create contradictory expectations

# 30. Foundational Audience Summary

ODRISC serves two connected audiences through one website.

The Patient experience helps women understand, prepare, monitor, participate, and communicate more effectively throughout the pregnancy journey.

The Provider experience helps healthcare professionals review, interpret, recognize, monitor, collaborate, and implement longitudinal maternal and fetal intelligence within care workflows.

The website must respect the different needs of each audience while reinforcing one shared principle:

> ODRISC helps patients and healthcare professionals understand how a pregnancy is evolving over time.
